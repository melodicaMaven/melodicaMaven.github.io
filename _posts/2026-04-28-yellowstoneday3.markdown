Today I woke up feeling much better. I was glad the apparent sickness didn't last long, since we planned on heading into Yellowstone to check out some of the major attractions.

The park's west entrance was about a 90 minute drive from the rental, so we stopped along the way to grab coffee and snacks in town. Not many places were open, and it was a bit hard to tell if "closed for the season" meant "closed for summer" or "closed for winter" at times. Apparently, none of the park gets plowed through the winter months, but snowmobiles are allowed. Rentals thrive through the winter, and some places are only open from November to April. Most, if not all of the trails in and around Yellowstone have long yellow/orange reflective poles along their edges to mark snowmobile paths where the roads and cliffs aren't visible due to snowfall.

On the other hand, there are a lot of summer activities in and around Yellowstone: hunting, fishing, camping, and so on. Ergo, some locations across the mountain towns are only open from May to October. We got caught in the inbetween for our trip, being here the last week of April.

As we got to the Yellowstone west entrance, the park ranger manning the ticket station gave us a map with the roads schedule. Apparently, much of the park's east side roads were still closed until May 1st for the northeast and south end, and May 8th for the roads around the lake. This wasn't a problem though, since we only intended to hit Old Faithful and Mammoth Springs.

(image of the map)

Old Faithful was up first. As mentioned previously, this was our first time heading into Yellowstone proper, and it was a surreal experience. It is a very driveable area with decent roads, but just off the pavement is the vast, untamed wilderness of the Rockies and Yellowstone caldera. The roads in were lined with pine, rivers, and meadows. Bison were a common sight during our trip, sometimes very close to (or in) the roadways.

(images from West Entrance)

Since Old Faithful was a major attraction, and probably the one Yellowstone is most famous for, it was paved road all the way there--no hiking out required. The parking lot can't be more than a quarter mile from the actual geyser, which could be seen steaming as we pulled in. Many folks from around the world were gathered around, since we arrived within a few minutes of the eruption window's start time. While we waited, the ravens kept us entertained. Though this certainly wasn't allowed, a young girl was feeding Oreos to a couple ravens. They were funny little things, coming up close to her and talking as if they were asking for more.

(images of the geyser and ravens)

We only needed to wait about 10 minutes for Old Faithful to erupt. It had a couple of small spurts before the main eruption, and the main eruption lasted around 2 minutes. I was recording for the entire wait beforehand, and was able to get it on video, and of course, a couple of pictures for good measure.

(images and video of Old Faithful)

After catching the eruption, we were free to toodle around. We hit the souvenir shop nearby, and I grabbed a poster and a wooden bison ornament to commemorate the visit. We then hit the road to backtrack up to Mammoth.

On the way to Mammoth, we passed the Grand Prismatic Spring, and decided to pop in for a lap on the boardwalk. Because it was still quite cold, around 32F, much of the pools were obscured by steam. It was still worth the visit, and we caught plenty of glimpses of the deep blue waters and multicolored edges due to the wind.

(images of the Prismatic Spring)

After the Prismatic Spring, we made another surprise stop: Gibbon Falls. This stop was right on the roadway, with the falls visible from the street. It was almost canyon-like, the tuff cliffs rising sharply on one side of the road, and the falls over the side of the other. We walked down to the lower outlook and back, then back on the road we went.

(images of Gibbon Falls)

A long drive later, we finally made it to Mammoth Springs. The mountains on the way in and those making up the springs were much different in their appearance, since we were now quite far out from the caldera rim. Like the Prismatic Spring, the ground here almost looked bleached and pock-marked. Vegetation stayed low to the ground, and was few and far between. Most of what could be seen in the springs areas was moss, likely due to the temperature in the region, acidity of the water, and sulfur in the air.

This area was much more vertical, but still decked out with walkways. The town of Mammoth could be seen from the upper region we entered from, and warm spring water flowed from the terraces all the way down. The sulfur smell was most potent here, and the walkways were lined with warnings not to leave the path due to the thin crust.

We didn't make it all the way down, as the day was already growing long. The elevation and cold got to me as well, with the air thinner at 7,000ft and the cold stealing what little breath I had left. That being said, I don't regret making the trek. The light dusting of snow and heavy steam from the springs added to the already picturesque scenery.

(images of Mammoth Springs)

I was completely spent by the time we made it back up the walkways to the car, and passed out for around 45 minutes of the drive home. I woke back up around the time we were leaving Yellowstone, and grabbed a couple of pictures on the way out since the bison were really keen on walking alongside the cars today.

(images of the road to West Entrance)

Though it flurried a few times while we were in the park, we thought we were done with snow for the rest of the trip based on the forecast. That, of course, didn't stop Mother Nature from giving us another dusting back at the rental for good measure. 

It was my turn to cook the family a meal, so I cooked up quite possibly the largest batch of fried rice I've ever attempted to make. It took a long while, and I almost botched it since I didn't think to thaw the frozen veggies beforehand, but I think it turned out well in the end. If I ever need to cook for seven again, I think I'll just do it in batches.

We finished off the night with some hot tub time, and we're looking to take a bit of a slower day tomorrow.
