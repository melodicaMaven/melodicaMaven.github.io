This will be the first in a series of journals detailing our family adventure into West Yellowstone and the surrounding area.

About a week before the trip, we began checking the weather forecast to start piecing together how to pack. This definitely caught us by surprise; the forecast was reading highs in the 40s and lows in the 20s. Weather can vary wildly in the Spring and Fall here in Maryland, so at the very least, it was nice to see it stay consistent. I generally tend to prefer colder weather anyway.

Our flight was scheduled to depart around 7:30AM EDT. We spent plenty of time prepping to leave the night before, so we were able to get up at 5AM to grab everything and go. It's been a while since I had driven so early in the morning, so we took it slow on the way to get coffee--had to keep my eyes out for deer.

We hit Dunkin' for lattes and a light breakfast before hitting the road for the airport. The drive was unremarkable, though I was a bit nervous since I had never parked in BWI's longterm lots before. Wasn't sure how frequent the buses ran or how long it would take to get to the terminal, but we found a spot near the entrance and hopped on the shuttle minutes later.

Security was quicker than I was expecting, though I shouldn't have been surprised since it was still very early. We made it to our gate with no issues, though our flight was delayed by about 15 minutes.

The 15 minutes stretched to 30, and the attendant at the gate let us know that they were calling in a flight attendent from standby, since they ran into some issues on the previous flight. I don't recall exactly how long we waited, but we weren't particularly stressed since there was a 3 hour layover waiting for us at Denver before heading to Bozeman.

I slept for most of the flight, as I typically do. It was about 3 hours from Baltimore to Denver, so I was able to catch up on some sleep from the early rising.

Denver was a large airport, and like most airports, was quite beautiful. We passed a sort of atrium on the way to our next gate, where you could see all three levels: the plane train/subway area on the bottom, the main terminals (which is the level we were on), and the observation deck up above. There was a large dome of skylights above, and a model plane suspended in the open area.

We found our next gate, had lunch, and hung out for the duration of the layover. A friend and I chatted over Discord about how the Denver airspace was infamous for its low visibility in its frequent haze and low cloud ceilings, as well as the high traffic that comes into its relatively few runways. In the hours we were there, I was not able to see through the clouds to the sky.

We boarded, pushed back, and our captain let us know it was about a 20 minute wait to the runway. Outside our window, you could see a line of over a dozen similarly sized planes lined up, waiting for their turn for takeoff. There was something amusing about seeing that long line of giant 737 jets, most of them being Amazon Prime planes. We watched as they lined up on the runway, took off, and disappeared into the clouds one by one.

Finally, our turn. Another uneventful but short flight, this one only around an hour. Visibility was much lower flying into Bozeman due to the haze and snow, but once we disembarked, the view from inside the airport was beautiful.

The Bozeman airport prides itself on the "mountain charm" that Big Sky Country is known for. It was a small airport ungoing rennovations, with only two concourses of gates (A and B). Even still, the interior was lined with natural fir and pine and embraced its open, high ceilings. There were many large windows, offering fantastic views of the runways and distant snowy mountains. The shops were cozy, and similarly embraced the natural wood and stone finishes. A gas fireplace was going, right there in the middle of the terminal.

The family picking us up somehow arrived perfectly on time, as we were walking down to the airport baggage area. We hopped in the van and headed over to the AirBnb. It was a 2 hour drive, but the last long wait before we could finally get settled in for vacation.

The drive was like nothing I had ever seen, though I knew I would love it. The area in and around this part of Montana is known for its wide meadows, coniferous forests, and alpine slopes. We saw deer, elk, lots of ravens, and even a bison along the roadways on the way to the rental. It snowed on and off throughout the drive.

I had not seen the AirBnb prior to pulling into the driveway, since the parents did most of the planning for this particular trip. It was a sort of large log cabin-styled house out in Island Park, Idaho, and was *much* larger on the inside than it looked on the outside. It has four bedrooms (and additional beds), three living areas, and four bathrooms spread amongst the three floors.

The main (middle) floor was the most open. When walking in, the first thing that stood out was the use of unpainted, natural wood on nearly every wall, railing, door, and shelf. This floor had the laundry closet, master bedroom and bathroom, kitchen, dining room, main living room, a TV, and gas fireplace. Since the house was built on a slope, there was also a double glass door leading out to the wraparound porch. The living room, kitchen, and dining room, all made up a larger floor-to-roof greatroom with large windows up above.

This takes us upstairs, where a loft overlooks the greatroom. The loft has another small sitting space, which I find quite nice as I sit here writing. As I write, my partner's younger brother and his husband sit up here with me, overlooking the parents and my partner in the greatroom as they watch a hockey game. Two bedrooms and a bathroom are up here, and the view of the treetops from the large greatroom window is incredible.

Lastly, the downstairs area. This area has a large den with a sitting area and TV, spare beds, access to the patio beneath the porch, hot tub, utility closet, bathroom, and the last bedroom. This bedroom was mine and my partner's during the trip, and was the only one with a TV and space heater. Which was nice, since we did not have any heating at the time.

After getting unpacked and settled in, we had dinner, and my partner and I, older brother, and younger brother all threw on swimsuits to hang out in the hot tub. Our younger brother's husband initially didn't want to try it since they can be bacterial breeding grounds, but he got in as well with a bit of convincing. This was my first time in an outdoor hot tub, and it was an incredible feeling being in it as a light snow fell around us.

Given the long travel day, I was exhausted, but ready for the rest of the week in this very beautiful part of the country.
