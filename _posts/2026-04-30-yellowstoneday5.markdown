Since some of the last attractions that we wanted to see in Yellowstone were along roads that were still closed, we decided to use this day to check out Grand Teton. Many of the roads were still closed there as well, but there were some hiking spots and a visitor center open to visit.

The drive was a bit long, over 2 hours, so we stopped in Ashton for breakfast along the way. Ashton was a small town surrounded by large grain and potato fields with a couple of cute little shops towards the center. We stopped in one of these shops, it was a gift shop with a coffee bar. When we opened the door we met Brewer: an old black lab who loves to greet all the guests. I found a unique handmade clay plate with an etching of the Teton range on it.

The Teton mountains could be seen for miles in all directions, jutting out from the horizon over the otherwise flat farmland. The roads were rough and windy, the speed limits were high and farmers' pickups blew past us, but it was a normal drive nonetheless.

The open farmlands were a hot spot for birds of prey--we saw about a dozen raptors on the way there and back. Many of them were Red-Tail Hawks, though we caught glimpses of Bald Eagles, Peregrine Falcons and Turkey Vultures as well. Given that beginner falconry birds (Kestrels and Red-Tailed Hawks) are so common in this region, and that there's a need for pest control in agricultural lands, I imagine falconry must be a popular activity here.

To get to Grand Teton from where we left, we had to head through a narrow pass in the Rockies. This area was around a town called Jackson's Hole--a much larger town than Ashton, and at a much higher elevation. Along the mountain pass were tracks from skiers, which was a bit surprising. Once we arrived in Grand Teton, we made a point to check out the visitor center. We snagged a map to find an easy hike along one of the open roads, and I found a poster and pocket birding guide as well. The visitor center featured many exhibits on the formation of the Teton range, the wildlife and the various biomes, and the native peoples who inhabited these lands prior to the arrival of settlers.
